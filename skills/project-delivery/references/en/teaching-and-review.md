# Explain, discuss and review

Start with the customer, goal, current journey, pain, final deliverable, current project stage and largest unknown. Use a normal example end to end before exceptions and technical detail. An owner's offhand product idea is not automatically a customer requirement.

Explain three layers: business describes who does what; system describes modules, data and interfaces; technical explains choices, limitations, cost and proof. For an important term, answer what it is, why this project needs it, how it will be used, what can fail and how to test it. Do not invent the expansion of an undefined acronym.

Use a small readable overview, then focused subflows. Business diagrams show roles, inputs, rules, outputs and exceptions; architecture diagrams show boundaries, data directions, deployment and dependencies. Avoid an unreadable all-in-one map.

Tables suit comparisons, measurements and mappings; use prose for rationale and explanations. Use business names before internal IDs. Internal records, owner explanations and customer submissions may have different levels of detail while sharing the same facts and evidence.

At a natural point, invite the owner to explain inputs, processing, approval and failure conditions in their own words. This locates explanation gaps; it is not a test or an approval ceremony. Continue independent work if a response is not needed.

Prepare a one-minute overview, a five-minute explanation and deeper answers to likely technical questions. Distinguish documented requests, proposals and untested assumptions in each version.

Before a meeting, provide current agreement, the few decisions needed, questions requiring customer facts or permissions, likely challenges and supporting answers. Investigate technical questions first when authorized. Explain why each requested item matters, its suggested contact, format, priority and fallback. Unconfirmed contacts remain suggested roles, not real departments.

After the meeting, record decisions, unresolved issues, owners, dates, sources and impacts. An AI interpretation of motives or attitudes is not a quote. Drafting a message does not authorize sending it.

Review from business, engineering, delivery, commercial and reader perspectives. One person can apply these in sequence; separate agents require actual authorization and a worthwhile benefit. Check actual pain and measures; data, rules and dependencies; people and calendar; scope and cost completeness; and clear terms, sources, state and formatting.

Report specific findings and corrections instead of a generic claim that a professional review passed. Missing critical evidence, unavailable people or hard-constraint violations must remain visible, not disappear through polished wording.
